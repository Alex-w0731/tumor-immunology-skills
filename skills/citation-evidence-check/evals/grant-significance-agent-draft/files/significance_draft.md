# Significance (draft 2, written with an AI writing assistant)

PD-1 blockade produces durable responses in several solid tumours: in a phase 1 trial of an
anti-PD-1 antibody, objective responses occurred in 40% of patients with non-small-cell lung
cancer [1]. Benefit concentrates in T-cell-inflamed tumours, and a pan-tumour IFN-γ-related gene
expression profile predicts clinical benefit from pembrolizumab [2]. Within the tumour, TIGIT
suppresses antitumour immunity mainly through regulatory T cells rather than CD8+ T cells [3],
and CXCL13+ exhausted CD8+ T cells predict response to anti-PD-1 therapy in hepatocellular
carcinoma [4]. Finally, oral Bifidobacterium improves the efficacy of PD-L1 blockade in patients
with melanoma [5], which motivates Aim 3.
