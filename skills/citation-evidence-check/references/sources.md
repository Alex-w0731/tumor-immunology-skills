## Language models and references

- **[Walters2023]** Walters WH, Wilder EI. Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT. Sci Rep. 2023;13(1):14045. doi:10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5. PMID: 37679503.
  Abstract: 636 citations in 84 short literature reviews; 55% of GPT-3.5 and 18% of GPT-4 citations were fabricated, and 43% and 24% of the real ones contained substantive citation errors.
- **[Bhattacharyya2023]** Bhattacharyya M, Miller VM, Bhattacharyya D, Miller LE. High rates of fabricated and inaccurate references in ChatGPT-generated medical content. Cureus. 2023. doi:10.7759/cureus.39238. PMID: 37337480.
  Abstract: of 115 references in 30 ChatGPT-3.5 medical papers, 47% were fabricated, 46% authentic but inaccurate and 7% authentic and accurate; an incorrect PMID was the most common error (93% of papers).
- **[Chelli2024]** Chelli M, Descamps J, Lavoué V, et al. Hallucination rates and reference accuracy of ChatGPT and Bard for systematic reviews: comparative analysis. J Med Internet Res. 2024;26:e53164. doi:10.2196/53164. PMID: 38776130.
  Abstract: a reference counted as hallucinated when two of title, first author and year were wrong; rates were 39.6% (GPT-3.5), 28.6% (GPT-4) and 91.4% (Bard), and the authors conclude that model-generated references need thorough validation.
- **[Peters2025]** Peters U, Chin-Yee B. Generalization bias in large language model summarization of scientific research. R Soc Open Sci. 2025;12(4):241776. doi:10.1098/rsos.241776. PMID: 40309181.
  Abstract: 4900 summaries from 10 models; even when prompted for accuracy most models generalised beyond the original text (26% to 73% of cases for three of them), and model summaries were about five times more likely than human-written ones to contain broad generalisations (OR 4.85, 95% CI 3.06 to 7.70).

## Citation and quotation accuracy in the biomedical literature

- **[Jergas2015]** Jergas H, Baethge C. Quotation accuracy in medical journal articles: a systematic review and meta-analysis. PeerJ. 2015;3:e1364. doi:10.7717/peerj.1364. PMID: 26528420.
  Abstract: 28 studies; pooled quotation error rates of 25.4% in total (95% CI 19.5 to 32.4) and 11.9% major (8.4 to 16.6); the authors recommend that authors declare they have checked cited material.
- **[Mogull2017]** Mogull SA. Accuracy of cited "facts" in medical research articles: a review of study methodology and recalculation of quotation error rate. PLoS One. 2017;12(9):e0184727. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0184727. PMID: 28910404.
  Abstract: recalculated quotation error rate 14.5% (95% CI 10.5 to 18.6), computed per quotation examined rather than per reference, hence lower than earlier estimates of 20 to 25%; the definitions the skill uses for claim support: major error = the source fails to substantiate, is unrelated to, or contradicts the assertion; minor error = oversimplification, overgeneralisation or trivial inaccuracy; improper secondary (indirect) citation is counted separately (10.4%). Which statements fall under "minor" (species, causal verb, small-sample rates) is this skill's mapping, not Mogull's.
- **[Greenberg2009]** Greenberg SA. How citation distortions create unfounded authority: analysis of a citation network. BMJ. 2009;339:b2680. doi:10.1136/bmj.b2680. PMID: 19622839.
  Abstract: in a citation network on one biomedical claim, citation bias, amplification by papers with no data on the claim, and invention (a hypothesis turned into fact through citation alone) created unfounded authority, including in NIH grant text. Basis for tracing a claim to primary data.
- **[Hsiao2021]** Hsiao TK, Schneider J. Continued use of retracted papers: temporal trends in citations and (lack of) awareness of retractions shown in citation contexts in biomedicine. Quant Sci Stud. 2021;2(4):1144-1169. doi:10.1162/qss_a_00155. PMID: 36186715.
  Abstract: retracted papers indexed in PubMed continued to be cited, and only 722 of 13,252 post-retraction citation contexts (5.4%) acknowledged the retraction.
- **[Boutron2018]** Boutron I, Ravaud P. Misrepresentation and distortion of research in biomedical literature. Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 2018;115(11):2613-2619. doi:10.1073/pnas.1710755115. PMID: 29531025.
  Abstract (literature review): defines spin (reporting that fails to faithfully reflect the nature and range of the findings) and describes its forms, from "beautified" methods to misinterpreted results. Basis for checking a claim against the results rather than a conclusion sentence. The full text was not accessible, so no claim about where spin is most common is taken from it.
- **[Brierley2022]** Brierley L, Nanni F, Polka JK, et al. Tracking changes between preprint posting and journal publication during a pandemic. PLoS Biol. 2022;20(2):e3001285. doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3001285. PMID: 35104285.
  Abstract: among bioRxiv and medRxiv preprints later published, the abstract conclusions of 7.2% of non-COVID-19 and 17.2% of COVID-19 preprints underwent a discrete change, mostly not a qualitative one.
- **[Mestas2004]** Mestas J, Hughes CCW. Of mice and not men: differences between mouse and human immunology. J Immunol. 2004;172(5):2731-2738. doi:10.4049/jimmunol.172.5.2731. PMID: 14978070.
  Abstract (review): lists differences between mouse and human innate and adaptive immunity (leukocyte subsets, Toll-like and NK receptors, Fc receptors, cytokines, costimulation, chemokines) that should be considered when mice model human disease. Basis for stating species when a claim is generalised.

## Guidelines and service documentation (dated web pages; re-check when they change)

- **[ICMJE2026]** International Committee of Medical Journal Editors. Recommendations for the Conduct, Reporting, Editing, and Publication of Scholarly Work in Medical Journals. Updated January 2026. https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/
  Page text ("Preparing a Manuscript for Submission", References; re-read in review, November 2026): provide direct references to original research sources whenever possible; review articles do not always reflect original work accurately; a cited preprint must be marked as a preprint; referencing AI-generated material as the primary source is not acceptable; authors should be able to attest that the references cited support the associated statement; verify references against a bibliographic source such as PubMed or the original; authors must check that no reference cites a retracted article except when referring to the retraction; for MEDLINE-indexed journals ICMJE considers PubMed the authoritative source on retractions. "Defining the Role of Authors and Contributors", AI-assisted technology: humans are responsible for AI-assisted material, which can be authoritative-sounding yet incorrect, incomplete or biased.
- **[NLM2026]** U.S. National Library of Medicine. Errata, Retractions, and Other Linked Citations in PubMed. Last reviewed 10 February 2026. https://www.nlm.nih.gov/bsd/policy/errata.html
  Page text: the publication type Retracted Publication is assigned to the retracted article, whose citation links to the notice with "Retraction in"; the notice itself carries the type Retraction Notice and links back with "Retraction of"; errata and expressions of concern are linked the same way ("Erratum in", "Expression of concern in"), and a republished correction carries the type Corrected and Republished Article. These are the fields verify_references.py reads. Observed in November 2026: the PubMed record of the notice for Kurtulus 2015 (record 38949028) carries "Retraction Notice"; older records may still carry the former type "Retraction of Publication", so the script accepts both.
- **[Crossref2026a]** Crossref. REST API documentation and Tips for using the Crossref REST API. Accessed November 2026. https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/rest-api/ and https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/rest-api/tips-for-using-the-crossref-rest-api/
  Page text: back off when HTTP 429 (rate limited) responses appear; a contact address in the request joins the "polite pool", which this skill's script deliberately does not use (no e-mail address is sent).
- **[Crossref2026b]** Crossref. Retraction Watch (Metadata Retrieval documentation). Accessed November 2026. https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/retraction-watch/
  Page text: Crossref acquired the Retraction Watch database in September 2023 and made it public; retractions appear in the REST API with a source field of publisher or retraction-watch. Observed in November 2026: the retracted article's own record lists them under `updated-by`, the notice's record under `update-to`; the script reads both.
- **[NCBI2017]** NCBI Insights. New API Keys for the E-utilities. 2 November 2017. https://ncbiinsights.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2017/11/02/new-api-keys-for-the-e-utilities/
  Page text: an API key is needed only above three requests per second from one IP address; basis for the script's default 0.4 s spacing.
- **[NCBI2026]** NCBI. Entrez Programming Utilities Help (NCBI Bookshelf). Accessed November 2026. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK25501/
  URL only: the Bookshelf served a browser check to scripted requests, so the text was not re-read (again in review, November 2026). The skill takes only the endpoint names (esearch, esummary, efetch) and field tags ([1au], [dp], [ti], [pt]), each confirmed by a live request in November 2026. Because the current page could not be read, the three-requests-per-second limit rests on [NCBI2017] alone.

## Method

- **[Dice1945]** Dice LR. Measures of the amount of ecologic association between species. Ecology. 1945;26(3):297-302. doi:10.2307/1932409.
  Metadata: the coefficient 2|A and B| / (|A| + |B|), used by verify_references.py on title word sets. The 0.9 and 0.6 thresholds are this skill's heuristics, not from this paper.
